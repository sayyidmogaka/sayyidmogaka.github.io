Sayyid Mogaka

I am a computational geoscientist and machine learning engineer with an academic background in applied mathematics and petroleum engineering. My work sits at the intersection of mathematical modelling, geoscience, scientific computing and AI.

For my master’s thesis, supervised by Associate Professor Ilia Safonov, I applied image-processing techniques to micro-CT scans of reservoir rock to compute geodesic tortuosity and compare the computational efficiency of shortest-path algorithms.

I am actively seeking PhD opportunities where I can further explore the intersection of applied mathematics, computational modelling, geoscience and machine learning.

Research interests

My work and projects are multidisciplinary, drawing on applied mathematics, fundamental physics, petroleum engineering and computer science. I am particularly interested in:

  • Multiphase flow in porous media. Understanding the physics governing flow, using image-based characterisation to investigate pore-scale features that control it, and connecting processes from the pore scale to the continuum scale.
  • AI for Science (AI4Science). Exploring how machine learning and AI can complement traditional scientific methods, deepen our understanding of complex physical systems and help address problems that are computationally expensive or otherwise difficult to tackle.
  • Scientific computing. Leveraging high-performance computing (HPC) architectures and parallel solvers to simulate multiphysics processes.

Curiosity beyond research

I enjoy exploring complex topics, including those outside my main research areas. With AI as a tool for learning and building, I can connect insights from different fields and turn interesting questions into projects. So, if you come across something in my portfolio that seems unrelated to my research, I probably ventured into unfamiliar territory, found something fascinating and decided to explore it.
